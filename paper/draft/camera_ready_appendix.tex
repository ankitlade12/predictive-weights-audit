\section{Nonlinearity decision rule and complete cell results}
\label{app:gates}

\paragraph{Data and splits.}
The controlled generator has $K\in\{10,20\}$ variables, $T\in\{300,600\}$
retained observations, 300 burn-in steps, self coefficient $.35$, edge
coefficient $.55$, and innovation standard deviation $.08$. Its only
cross-variable edges are $0\to1$ at lag 3 and $2\to3$ at lag 4; these are
unchanged across strengths. The nonlinearity is scaled using 64-point
Gauss--Hermite quadrature under a fixed Gaussian AR(1) reference, independently
of experimental data and seeds. This controls reference marginal scale;
it does not guarantee equality of every finite-sample distribution.

Each raw series is split chronologically into 80\% training and 20\% test
observations. Per-variable means and scales are fitted on the training split
only. Lag windows are constructed separately within each split. Lasso uses
the final 25\% of the training series for prediction-validation selection
from $\{10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}$, then refits on the entire
training series. This is the existing multi-output \texttt{MultiTaskLasso}
baseline; its penalty couples coefficients across outcome variables.
``Lasso'' in the paper refers to this implementation when outcome masks are
absent. With intervention target masks, regressions are fitted per outcome.
No graph labels select the penalty. The linear bottleneck uses width 32,
80 epochs, batch size 64, learning rate $.001$, weight decay $.0001$, and
endpoint sparsity coefficient $.001$. Width 32 exceeds $K$ here, so a strict
output-rank bottleneck is not imposed in this experiment; factorization and
optimization/regularization effects must not be conflated with rank restriction.

\paragraph{Metrics and inference.}
The primary edge universe is the $5K(K-1)$ off-diagonal lag--target--source
entries, with two positive labels. Edge prevalence is therefore
$2/[5K(K-1)]$: $.004444$ for $K=10$ and $.001053$ for $K=20$.
AUROC and AUPRC are computed on that universe; AUPRC uses trapezoidal
integration of the precision--recall curve, not average precision. For tied
scores its interpolation can differ from a prevalence-valued random-ranking
reference; the prevalence gate is a heuristic floor, not a calibrated null
test. Prediction MSE averages squared errors over held-out lagged rows and
variables in training-standardized units.

Data seeds 60000--60009 are paired between methods and reused across cells;
model seeds 70000--70009 accompany them. Reuse across strengths does not
create additional independent replications. For each cell, the effect is
the mean of ten paired AUROC differences. The 95\% percentile interval
resamples those ten pairs 10,000 times. One-sided Wilcoxon tests use the
alternative of positive bottleneck-minus-Lasso differences; all-zero
differences are marked unevaluable and contribute a conservative placeholder
$p=1$ to the correction family. They cannot pass the significance gate.
Holm adjustment is across the 24 cell tests.
The AUPRC and MSE gates compare cell means and are descriptive thresholds,
not additional significance tests. Neither the original gates nor their
multiplicity family was changed after review.

Writing $\overline{A}_B,\overline{A}_L$ for mean AUPRC and
$\overline{E}_B,\overline{E}_L$ for mean test MSE, a positive cell requires
\[
\overline{\Delta}\geq .03,\quad L_{95}>0,\quad p_{\mathrm{Holm}}<.05,
\quad\overline{A}_B>\overline{A}_L,\quad\overline{A}_B>\pi_K,
\quad\overline{E}_B\leq1.10\overline{E}_L.
\]
The regime rule is
\[
\exists T:\quad
\left|\{s\in\{.2,.3,.4\}:\ \forall K\in\{10,20\},\
\mathrm{pass}(K,T,s)\}\right|\geq2.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:gates} gives every cell, including the linear and fully
nonlinear controls. Six positive cells confined to $K=20,T=300$ do not meet
this rule.

\paragraph{Post-review diagnostic.}
Re-fitting the original Lasso configurations reproduces selected penalties,
AUROC, and held-out MSE for all 240 runs. The diagnostic counts nonzero
off-diagonal lag scores, without inferring whether self coefficients are
zero. All 60 $K=20,T=300$ fits select $\alpha=1$ and have zero off-diagonal
scores. At $K=10,T=300$, six fits (one data seed across all six strengths)
select $\alpha=1$ and have zero scores; the other 54 select $.1$. All 120
$T=600$ fits select $.1$. These observations locate the uninformative ranking
in the prediction-selected comparator; they do not establish a universal
regularization mechanism or justify selecting penalties by graph accuracy.

\section{Post-review replication and additional baselines}
\label{app:followup}

\paragraph{Scope and pairing.}
The follow-up was specified after receiving reviewer feedback and is
exploratory. The ten Lorenz-96 trajectories share the original graph and
settings ($K=10,T=1500,F=10$, integration step $.01$, subsampling factor 10,
500 discarded integration steps), but use independently drawn initial states
from seeds 81000--81009. They do not vary graph topology, forcing, noise,
or sampling interval. Full-series standardization matches the submitted
graph-recovery benchmark; these are not held-out forecasting results.
Each estimator receives the same exported standardized array, verified by
SHA-256 across environments, with lag horizon 3. All original estimator
settings are unchanged. The bottleneck's three initialization scores are
averaged within each trajectory, leaving ten units for inference.

Means and 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals in Table~\ref{tab:replication}
use 10,000 trajectory resamples with seed 82000. Paired differences are
formed within each trajectory before resampling. Intervals are marginal,
not simultaneous; no multiplicity-adjusted superiority claim is made.
We retain every planned trajectory and report package warnings and failures
in the machine-readable artifacts. A failed or missing method/trajectory
prevents generation of the complete comparison table.

\paragraph{Additional baseline configuration.}
The optional baselines use an isolated Python 3.10.19 environment with
CausalNex 0.12.1 and LiNGAM 1.11.0; its exact installed package versions
are included in the archive. DYNOTEARS \citep{pamfil2020dynotears} uses
$p=3$, $\lambda_W=\lambda_A=.1$, maximum 100 iterations, acyclicity tolerance
$10^{-8}$, and weight threshold zero. VAR-LiNGAM
\citep{hyvarinen2010var} uses maximum lag 3, BIC lag selection within that
horizon, pruning enabled, and random state zero. Their package-default
settings are not graph-label tuned. This comparison therefore has a common
maximum lag horizon, not necessarily the same selected lag order or tuning
budget. Contemporaneous coefficients are excluded from scoring; only
positive-lag, off-diagonal coefficients are aggregated by maximum absolute
value in $[\mathrm{target},\mathrm{source}]$ orientation.

On the original seed-0 Lorenz-96 array, the added baselines give AUROC
\DynotearsOriginal{} (DYNOTEARS) and \LingamOriginal{} (VAR-LiNGAM).
They were not part of the submitted Table~\ref{tab:real}; that historical
comparison is preserved. Neither method is run on the intervention or
nonlinearity conditions in this revision. Their linear structural-model
assumptions (including non-Gaussian independent innovations for VAR-LiNGAM)
are not established for the subsampled Lorenz-96 trajectories. Their scores
are therefore bounded empirical comparisons, not proofs of causal validity.
