\begin{abstract}
Predictive models can expose weight patterns that resemble scientific
dependency graphs. We study one such candidate: the endpoint score
$S=|W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}|$ of a next-step time-series
predictor. Rather than introduce another causal-discovery competitor, we ask
which controls are required before a readable predictive weight can be
interpreted causally. Correcting matrix orientation and matching lag horizons
changes Lorenz-96 PCMCI AUROC from a previously reported $0.986$ to $0.839$.
Real observational
benchmarks provide only literature-derived climate labels or heuristic
finance labels, whereas Lorenz-96 is a simulation with an exact graph.
Target-aware intervention controls show small and semantics-dependent
bottleneck differences; under random forcing, all four tested methods show
positive gains, while a direct Granger--bottleneck contrast differs only at
$K=20$. Finally,
a pre-specified nonlinearity confirmation yields the same advantage at every
nonlinearity level for one finite-sample setting and no advantage after
doubling the sample size. The apparent ``nonlinear regime'' is therefore
consistent with a finite-sample/dimensional regularization effect. Prior
validated architecture controls found that endpoint scores recover planted
dependencies in controlled processes, but a linear bottleneck matched or
exceeded Mamba across all four reported datasets. Our contribution is a
reusable falsification protocol and responsible-interpretation checklist for
interpretability-based discovery. This negative study shows how plausible
causal stories can survive repeated experiments yet fail under estimand,
orientation, provenance, and baseline audits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A model explanation can be readable without identifying the data-generating
mechanism. This distinction is especially important in time series, where
predictive association, Granger predictability, intervention response, and
structural causation answer different questions
\citep{granger1969investigating,pearl2009causality}. Yet a compact weight
pattern is tempting scientific evidence: it is cheap, visually interpretable,
and available without fitting a dedicated causal model.

Our motivating case is a Mamba-style state-space forecaster
\citep{gu2023mamba}. With input and output projections
$W_{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times K}$ and
$W_{\mathrm{out}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}$, the score
\begin{equation}
  S = |W_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{in}}|,\qquad
  S_{ij}\ \text{interpreted as the strength of }j\rightarrow i
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
often looks graph-like after next-step prediction training. Earlier
exploration suggested architecture, intervention, and nonlinear-regime
stories. Those observations motivated this study, but are not its evidence:
we report only corrected local reruns under a fixed audit design.

This paper makes three contributions. First, it turns implementation and
estimand checks into a reusable protocol for auditing interpretability-based
scientific claims. Second, it applies the protocol to observational,
interventional, and controlled nonlinear experiments, preserving null and
contradictory results. Third, it gives a concise checklist and responsible-use
boundary. We do not propose Eq.~\ref{eq:score} as a causal-discovery method.

\paragraph{Validated prior architecture control.}
Separate from the corrected reruns below, prior validated experiments found
that endpoint scores recovered planted dependencies in controlled synthetic
processes, establishing readability in that limited setting. A linear
bottleneck matched or exceeded Mamba on all four reported datasets, rejecting
an SSM-specific interpretation.

\section{Audit Protocol}

The protocol treats a causal-looking interpretation as a sequence of claims
that can fail independently.

\paragraph{Representation semantics.}
Fix one score convention, here $S[\mathrm{target},\mathrm{source}]$, and test
it on planted directed, lagged edges. External packages require explicit
adapters: Tigramite stores values as
$[\mathrm{source},\mathrm{target},\mathrm{lag}]$. Strictly lagged evaluation
must also exclude contemporaneous matrices. These checks are scientific
controls, not software housekeeping: a transpose can reverse a leaderboard.

\paragraph{Protocol matching.}
Compared methods receive the same usable observations and lag horizon.
Independent trajectories remain separate so no lag window crosses a boundary.
Stochastic models use paired data and model seeds. Hyperparameters cannot be
selected using test graph labels.

\paragraph{Intervention-aware estimands.}
Every intervention segment records its target. When target $j$ is externally
forced, the outcome equation for $j$ is excluded from that segment for every
method; the remaining target equations are retained. We compare a combined
observational/interventional arm against an observational arm with the same
number of usable lagged rows. Constant clamps, soft forcing, and per-step
random forcing are separate experimental conditions, not interchangeable
instances of ``interventional data.''

\paragraph{Label provenance and inference.}
Each dataset is tagged as real observational or simulated, and each graph as
exact, literature-derived soft, or heuristic soft. Independent
data-generating seeds are the replication unit. We report paired mean effects,
bootstrap intervals, one-sided Wilcoxon tests, and Holm correction over each
declared family. Invalid all-zero tests remain null rather than being dropped.

\subsection{Intervention effects depend on the estimand and semantics}

We construct three arms: a small observational trajectory, a larger
observational arm, and a combined arm containing the small trajectory plus one
intervention segment per target. The latter two have equal usable rows. All
fits are segment-safe and target-aware.

Exp.~23 asks whether the bottleneck's intervention-specific gap exceeds
Lasso's or Ridge's. Across $K\in\{10,20\}$, $T=300$, and 10 independent
seeds, Holm-significant positive differences are only $0.012$--$0.021$
AUROC. Constant clamps produce no bottleneck relative advantage. Random and
soft forcing yield selected small differences, but no general or
bottleneck-specific intervention effect.

Exp.~25 applies target-aware random forcing to four methods. Its primary
estimand is
\[
 \Delta_m=\mathrm{AUROC}_m(\mathrm{combined})-
          \mathrm{AUROC}_m(\mathrm{size\mbox{-}matched\ observational}).
\]
Table~\ref{tab:int} reports paired means and bootstrap 95\% intervals.
All eight within-method tests reject after Holm correction
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.0078125$), so the gain is not bottleneck-specific.
However, an exploratory paired Granger-minus-Bottleneck contrast is null at
$K=10$ ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.278$) and positive at $K=20$
($+0.030$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.027$). The evidence therefore does not imply
method invariance. Target-aware Lasso also improves, so the earlier
target-corruption mechanism is unsupported.

\subsection{The apparent nonlinear regime is finite-sample regularization}

Exp.~27 fixes a Linear-BN ($d=32$, fixed sparsity coefficient) and tuned Lasso
before graph evaluation. It uses independent seeds and sweeps
$K\in\{10,20\}$, $T\in\{300,600\}$, and nonlinearity mixes
$\{0,.2,.3,.4,.6,1\}$. The pre-specified mild-regime rule requires at least
two of $.2,.3,.4$ to pass for every requested $K$ at one common $T$.
The artifact status is \texttt{isolated\_positive\_exploratory}; no $T$
satisfies the rule.

At $K=20,T=300$, Linear-BN minus Lasso AUROC is exactly $+0.50$ for
\emph{every} mix, including 0 and 1. At $K=10,T=300$, it is $+0.05$ for
every mix and no cell passes the gate. At $T=600$, both methods reach AUROC
1.00 for every mix. Because the difference is invariant to nonlinearity and
vanishes with more samples, we reject the mild-nonlinearity interpretation.
The isolated positive is consistent with finite-sample, dimensional
regularization in this generator.

\section{A Responsible Falsification Checklist}

Before treating an interpretable model component as a scientific discovery:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item test direction, lag, and diagonal conventions on planted examples;
  \item match data budgets, lag horizons, preprocessing, and random seeds;
  \item preserve trajectory boundaries;
  \item declare intervention semantics and encode targets in the estimand;
  \item include sparse and classical baselines under the same protocol;
  \item separate exact simulated graphs from literature and heuristic labels;
  \item fix variants before graph evaluation and use independent replicates;
  \item report effects, uncertainty, multiplicity correction, and failures;
  \item test whether an apparent regime varies along the proposed mechanism;
  \item state the strongest interpretation the evidence does \emph{not} support.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Responsible Use}

Our corrected experiments are deliberately narrow: two dimensions and sample
sizes for intervention and nonlinearity tests, one exact dynamical simulation,
and two observational datasets with soft labels. Deterministic benchmark runs
do not estimate method variance, and no result identifies a general causal
mechanism. Broader confounding, lag, noise, and intervention-coverage studies
are journal-scale work. DYNOTEARS and VAR-LiNGAM adapters were fixture-tested,
but their optional dependencies were unavailable for the corrected empirical
rerun.

These scores must not be used to prescribe scientific interventions, climate
policy, financial trades, or other consequential decisions. Climate and
finance labels are incomplete soft hypotheses, and Lorenz-96 is a simulation.
Readable predictive dependence can guide questions, but causal use requires
domain review, explicit assumptions, validated interventions, and evidence
outside the model being interpreted. We release contradictory results and
provenance metadata to reduce selective reporting and automation bias.

\section{Conclusion}

The endpoint bottleneck is a useful case study precisely because its causal
story looked plausible. Correct orientation, provenance, target-aware
estimands, intervention-semantic controls, and classical baselines remove the
strong interpretation. The remaining contribution is not a weaker causal
method, but a reusable way to falsify causal-looking interpretations before
they become scientific claims.
